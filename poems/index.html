A place for light

Seven poems. Read slowly.

breathe in
Begin
Soon in my own voice.
i

Who Am I?

Lord, who am I? I pray,Lord, send a sign my way –plain as a waking sight,stay with me through the night

here in my lucid sleep.Help me grow strong to leap.Open before me, Lord,field of Your game, its board,

now. Make Your move – and spillall of my plans at will.Ages of weight, not years –please overlook. My fears,

doubts, and their noisy sound –press “Start” to shut them down.Then, with the knots untied,on, with myself as guide…

Wider than mind can see,deeper than soul can be,“Breathe!” – the one law I own,carved in my heart like stone.

All that I have is love –one faith that soars above,older than truths I knew.Lord, who am I? – You…

Under every role – daughter, wife, mother – there is still you.

ii

The Road to Myself

I’ve followed my path many years – not just one, not just two;the road to myself never runs in a straight line – I roam.I swim or I fly or I walk – and beneath me, still new,unknown, it unrolls. Am I leaving, or heading back home?..I walk and I learn – even when there is someone I teach.I seek not the safe, quiet verge, but encounters en route.I weep in the night, and by day laugh beyond reason’s reach –all these are the steps, and without them I’d not find my root.At times it appears I am drawing my circles again,led on by the tangled-up thread of my doubts, I still go,held fast by a faith others grasp – though to me it’s not plain –or fate wrote my lot with a typo, at birth, long ago…I try to live knowing that death only comes on the dayyou wake up and don’t want to think through the question of why.As water of life, through the nerves of time, flowing away,my current slips past every circuit that’s steady and dry;it calms the uneasy, the trembling, with one burning flow,above all the cities and people I never once knew,and lures with the string of horizon, untouched, thin, and low –no memory, just an exhale of the roads I’ve come through.

Inner work never runs in a straight line: circles, doubts, tears and laughter are steps too.

iii

A human to a human

A human to a human is a gift,by fate prepared long before our start.

A human to a human is the flamethat’s lit to fill and warm the heart.

A human to a human is the light –both endless and a spark in time.

A human to a human leaves a tracethat travels far beyond what I can rhyme.

A human to a human is the roadwe walk and learn to call our only life.

A human for a human holds the truthwe keep and keep discovering inside.

A human to a human is the chanceof happiness we help to grow.

A human to a human is the God,when everything above is said and known.

Every person in our life comes with a gift – even the ones who are hard to love. A constellation can help you find it and bring it into the light.

iv

Teach Me to Cry

or hold the space bar

Oh, teach me how to cry – don’t fear.I’ll never waste this gift so dear,This precious salt the seas would claimIf only they could feel the same.

Teach me to weep when faith is gone,Or when a dream at last is won.Teach me to sleep – the childlike kind,Where dreams and dawn are intertwined,Where night and day divide so thin,You scarcely know where you have been.

Oh, tell me how a thousand songsLive word for word where heart belongs;And how a soul, with nothing owed,Can soar the sky – a bird unbowed.

Forbid me digging through my doubt,Blind to the strength that’s mine throughout.Teach me to pray – you know the art.He told me so. You touch His heart.

And soothe my pride, teach passion’s flameTo burn, yet not for passion’s name.But if my heart grows cold someday –Teach me no more… just hold, just stay.

Shadow is what we, and our families, were not allowed to feel. That is where the work begins.

v

Learning to Breathe with You

I am learning how to breathe in time with you – in tune,Falling in your arms the way the tide leans to the moon.When a sudden little nothing brushes past my sight –Comes from nowhere, comes from memory, comes from light.

By a fragrance, or a whisper, or the day’s soft hue,Or a word we speak together – only meant for two,I get wrapped inside the music of the moment’s call,Like a bow across the strings that tremble through it all.

And it follows where the path of beauty starts to rise,Through the silence, through the pauses, through the lullabies.Wait, before you lift away, before you take your flight –Let me see it, let me feel it, let me hold you tight.

Now I hear the quiet rustle fabric makes in air,Now I sense the stillness shifting in the space we share.Mind pretends it knows the way that every story ends,But I don’t believe “decisions” when the heart transcends.

When the fate we’re writing’s barely dry, still soft and new –When the work of spirit’s endless, always coming through.I am learning how to breathe in perfect time with you.And I will – you’ll see it – flawless, breath for breath, and true.

Trust instead of control: the mind only pretends to know how the story ends.

vi

Prayer

Lord, save us from ourselves – and stillhelp us remain as Your creation;when cursed, or hailed with loud ovation,keep us from dwelling on them; fill

our minds with love. Accept the painsof finding faith – our heart’s endeavor;deliver us from doubts that sever,and from the passions’ heavy chains.

Release us from the vain demandsof pride and envy, fear that slashesthe heart: that life will turn to ashesand joy will slip out of our hands.

Forgive us idle grief and care,the bustle of an empty pleasure.We need no more – no other treasure –and yet, how hard to end this prayer…

And so, in hope, to You abovewe speak – the candle burning, burning:fulfill the dreams, fulfill the yearningof those whom we so deeply love.

A wish for the people we love – the oldest wish in every family.

vii

Where Softness Is Allowed

Let there be rooms where softness stays –to breathe, to move, to feel, belong –where bodies, after years and daysof holding on, can hold a song.

For every woman taught to foldherself in half and keep it quiet –to stretch her arms out wide and bold,to taste her joy and not deny it;

to trust her roots, to claim the floor,remember strength she always had,to take up space – and then some more –and, unafraid, be simply glad.

For children – since no child was bornjust to survive another day –to roll downhill through summer corn,to let them run and dance and play,

to cup a beetle, ask it why,to climb, to wonder, to begin –to keep the whole unclouded skyof possibility within.

For those who carry war inside –a house that’s gone, a name, a street –a place where no one has to hidehow tired they are, and rest their feet;

where none will ask them to be strong,where softness doesn’t have to wait,and shoulders, braced for far too long,can finally put down the weight.

And let this peace be no one’s prize,not sold in studios with a view,not checked at borders, not one sizeof body. It belongs to you.
